# Instructor content review

## G36

The 16-step order supplied for the course is the answer key of the order test. Every step has been checked against the Bundeswehr manual A2-222/0-0-4741 “Das Gewehr G36” (§331 field strip, §333 bolt, §336 assembly and function check); the part identities and movement directions below are confirmed there.

### Edits made to the supplied wording

- “pistolveida” is written “pistoļveida”, as in other Latvian firearm texts. The step ID is unchanged, so scoring is unaffected.
- Earlier display edits are kept: `Salocīt laidi`, `aptveres ligzda` (instead of “magazīna”), `pastobrs`, `aizslēga atgriezējmehānisms` (from “Aizslēgatgriezējmehānismu”).

### Points for the instructor

1. **Order.** The manual removes the magazine well before folding the stock and does not unscrew the flash hider during a field strip. The course order is mechanically possible; confirm that it is the intended one.
2. **Unscored stages.** The learning mode adds the manual's safety check (remove the magazine, check the chamber) before disassembly and the function check after assembly. They are not part of the test.
3. **Pin storage.** The adjustable stock of the course rifle has three storage holes near the butt for the rear, centre and front pins. The tutorial recommends putting each removed pin there straight away: an "IETEIKUMS" note at both pin steps and beside the stage, and a close-up of the stock in the corner of the stage during the two pin steps only, where the rear and centre pins go into the rear and lower hole and the front pin into the front hole (the stock is folded behind the receiver then, so it cannot be shown in place). The animation lays the pins on the mat only as a visual cue. Which pin goes into which hole is a suggestion; the manual only says "in eine der Bohrungen".
4. **Sling.** The sling is hooked to the front swivel under the handguard and to the sling loop at the rear of the receiver, as on the course rifles.
5. **Pictures.** The course rifle is the G36 (first published here as G36C). The receiver, handguard, barrel and all other parts come from a field-strip photograph of a G36C that appears to be a replica with a two-piece receiver, so the front of the pictured rifle is shorter than on the G36 and it has the G36C carrying handle with a rail. The middle of the receiver is rebuilt from its own texture, and the pins, firing pin, gas piston, operating rod and barrel section are renders. Only the adjustable stock comes from a photograph of a Bundeswehr G36K A4, mirrored and scaled to fit. The field strip is the same on all G36 variants; small details of the pictured parts may differ from the course rifles.

## AK-4

No course order was supplied, so the AK-4 module follows the published Latvian procedure: **Jaunsarga rokasgrāmata**, chapter 12.2 “Triecienšautenes daļējā izjaukšana” (based on “Triecienšautene AK-4 (G-3)”, Rīga 2005). The step labels are the answer key of the AK-4 order test:

1. Atvienot laidi ar atgriezējmehānismu
2. Izņemt aizslēga rāmi ar aizslēgu
3. Atvienot pistoļveida rokturi ar šaušanas mehānismu
4. Noņemt pastobri
5. Izņemt drošinātāju-pārslēdzēju
6. Izņemt šaušanas mehānismu
7. Atvienot aizslēgu un belžņa ieliktni
8. Izņemt belzni un belžņa atsperi

The handbook's step 6 (“Atvienot šaušanas mehānismu no korpusa”) is split into steps 5 and 6, in the order the handbook describes the actions. The unscored safety check (magazine out, chamber check, control press) comes first, the function check (S, P, A) after assembly. How each part moves – pins pushed out sideways, stock straight back, the cocking handle pushing the bolt carrier out of the rear, the grip down, the handguard's front down and then forward, the selector turned anticlockwise to vertical, the trigger mechanism lifted by its hammer, the bolt turned and pulled off with the locking piece – is confirmed in the Bundeswehr manual ZDv 3/13 “Das Gewehr G3”, Nr. 319–323 (the AK-4 is the Swedish licence build of the G3).

### Points for the instructor

1. **Sources differ.** A Jaunsardze unit handout for the AK-4 (republished on spoki.lv) releases the grip before removing the bolt carrier, has an extra step “novieto stobru ar stobra kārbu” and tells the trainee *not* to do the control press. The module follows the handbook, which does the control press and has no such steps. The German manual also unhooks the sling and unscrews the flash hider; neither is part of the Latvian procedure, so neither is animated.
2. **Where the pins go.** The Latvian texts say only “izbīdīt uz sāniem” and “ievietot pagaidu vietā”. The animation follows the German manual: the two stock pins go into the hollow rivets near the butt, the grip and handguard pins back into their holes. The pins come out on the left side (their heads are on the left, as on other HK rifles); confirm on the course rifles.
3. **Selector markings.** The function check uses S, P and A as in the Latvian material; the photographed rifle is a German G3A3 with S, E, F. Other small details of the Swedish AK-4 (sights, markings) may differ from the photos.
4. **Pictures.** The parts come from a Wikimedia Commons collage of a G3A3 whose parts were photographed at different scales and some from the other side. They are rotated, mirrored (stock, grip) and scaled to match the picture of the assembled rifle in the same photo; the bolt carrier is a top view rebuilt as a side view, the magazine is stretched to side-view proportions, and the sizes of the small bolt parts are estimates. The movements are right; exact proportions inside the receiver are approximate.
5. **Technical data.** The theory questions use the handbook's values (1045 mm, 450 mm barrel, 4,5 kg, 20 rounds, 400 m, 500–650 rounds/min, 780–800 m/s, 4 grooves to the right, sight 200–500 m). The German manual gives slightly different figures for the G3 (e.g. 4,40 kg, sight 200–400 m, maximum range 3700 m instead of 4500 m) and the unit handout 4,7 kg; the questions avoid the figures the sources disagree on most (maximum range) but the mass and sight range should be confirmed for the course rifles.

## Munīcija

### Points for the instructor

1. **Calibre.** The site says the calibre is measured between opposite walls of the base bore (Melderis p. 65) and that the bullet is slightly larger (5,70 mm for 5,56 mm, C.I.P.). Melderis p. 64 also says the bullet is 0,1–0,15 mm *smaller* than the calibre; that sentence contradicts his own p. 65 and the C.I.P. data, so it is not used.
2. **Colour codes** are examples from German, US and NATO ammunition (green tip SS109/M855, red DM21 / orange M856 tracer, black AP, silver API) and the Swedish Home Guard photo (white-tipped tracer). The markings of the ammunition the course actually uses should be confirmed; the site tells trainees to check the packaging.
3. **Blanks.** "Never without the adapter", "issued only when nobody has live rounds" and "at least 10 m" come from the Bundeswehr G36 manual (Nr. 707, 708, 711). Latvian range rules may set other distances.
4. **Misfire.** The 10-second rule comes from MK noteikumi Nr. 494 (shooting ranges); the raised hand from the Jaunsarga rokasgrāmata (30.5). NBS range rules may add steps.
5. **Not included:** a post-shooting declaration such as "Man nav patronu, čaulu un to daļu" – no source was found for the exact wording; it can be added if the course uses one.
6. **Photos.** The sectioned cartridge is a Swiss 7,5 × 55 mm round with the powder removed (the structure is the same as the NATO rounds); the five-type photo shows German G3 ammunition, the headstamps US-made rounds.

## Ekipējums

Seven topics, 64 written questions and 16 marker questions (80 in total).

### Points for the instructor

1. **Patches.** Placement follows the current Cabinet regulation (MK noteikumi Nr. 26, 2020, annex 2, figures 106–108) for the regular forces. The 2012 Defence Ministry rules gave the Zemessardze field uniform a "ZEMESSARDZE" tape instead of the surname and the battalion number on the shoulder strap; the current regulation has no separate Zemessardze variant, so the site asks trainees to check in their unit. The Jaunsardze patches follow JC noteikumi Nr. 8-NOT (2021).
2. **Pocket contents** are the course's list as supplied by the trainee (kompass – kreisās piedurknes kabata; pierakstu blociņš ar pildspalvu – labās piedurknes kabata; apliecība – jakas kreisā krūšu kabata; taktiskie cimdi un balaklava – kreisā augšstilba kabata; labā augšstilba kabata – pēc izvēles; medpakete – kreisā apakšstilba kabata; atstarotājs un salvetes – labā apakšstilba kabata). The table's notes come from the 2012 appearance rules, the Jaunsardze rules and the VAM reminder; where no source says why a thing goes in that pocket, the note is left empty.
3. **Rules from 2012.** The reflective band on the traffic side, pens not clipped outside the uniform, the ID tag worn round the neck on exercises, headwear, bags and umbrellas come from AM noteikumi Nr. 18-NOT (2012). It is not certain that these rules are still in force; the site says so.
4. **Kit systems.** KIAS / KMPS / KSIP and the kit contents come from the supply norms in AM noteikumi Nr. 27-NOT (2015); check that they are still current. The VAM programme's terms KE and PKE are named but not defined, because no public definition was found.
5. **3-day bag.** No regulation with a packing order was found. The site shows the Jaunsarga rokasgrāmata principles (heaviest and least needed at the bottom, what the task needs on top, waterproof liner, nothing loose or noisy, load on the hips) and the 3-day kit list of the 2021 NBS march competition (sargs.lv). The course's own list and order should replace or extend this.
6. **Alfa soma** has no public definition; it is mentioned as a course term and not asked in the tests.
7. **First-aid kit.** The 17 items come from MK noteikumi Nr. 720, annex 2; the notes on what each item is for are a study aid. "Two tourniquets – one in the pouch, one on the uzkabe" is quoted from a Zemessardze first-aid instructor (sargs.lv, 2022). The place of the medpakete (left lower-leg pocket) is the course rule.
8. **IMUMS, COLD, hygiene kit** come from the VAM "Pamata militāro prasmju kursa atgādne" (the PDF names no publisher).
9. **Photos.** The patch figure is the regulation's own photo (face blurred in the original) with its printed labels removed. The other photos are US government and Creative Commons photos of Latvian soldiers.

## Theory questions (both weapons)

Each weapon now has a “Tehniskie dati un darbība” topic in the tests: 10 questions for the G36 from the Heckler & Koch G36 technical data (G36 column: 5,56 × 45 mm, 30 rounds, gas operation with a rotating bolt, 480 mm barrel, 1002 / 755 mm, about 750 rounds/min and 920 m/s, about 3630 g; the length and mass are for the standard folding stock, so confirm them for the course rifles with the adjustable stock) and 16 for the AK-4 from the sources above. Wrong answers use the other weapon's figures where they make good distractors (e.g. the G36's gas operation for the AK-4 and the other way round).

## Drill question bank (Ierinda)

The 100 questions are used as supplied (`src/drill/ierinda-bank.json`). Only the typography is adjusted when they are shown: the em dashes (—) are displayed as the Latvian en dash (–).

1. **Answer length gives the answer away.** In 71 of the 100 questions the correct answer is the longest option, and in 40 it is more than 1.6 times longer than the longest wrong answer: 01-04, 01-08, 02-02, 02-05, 02-08, 03-01, 03-02, 03-04, 03-06, 03-07, 03-09, 04-04, 04-06, 04-07, 04-08, 05-01, 05-02, 05-03, 05-04, 05-06, 05-09, 06-06, 06-10, 07-02, 07-03, 07-05, 07-07, 07-08, 07-09, 07-10, 08-05, 08-09, 08-10, 09-01, 09-02, 09-05, 09-07, 09-08, 10-01, 10-10. Always choosing the longest answer scores about 71 %, which passes at the 70 % pass mark. Lengthening the wrong answers (or shortening the correct ones) of these questions is recommended.
2. **Near-duplicates.** 01-09 / 02-01 (weapon safety check before drill), 06-07 / 06-08 (mirror images of each other) and 06-05 / 10-04 (“ATLIKT!”) ask almost the same thing. Chapter 10 repeats earlier chapters on purpose, so this may be intended.
3. **Wording to check.** “sānis” in the wrong answers of 05-03 and 07-07 (standard form: “sāņus”); 02-10 “saņemtas pavēles” reads more naturally as “saņemtās pavēles”; 08-05 uses “skaitīšanās”, 08-09 “saskaitīšanās” for the same procedure.

## Generated weapon questions (combined test)

The combined test adds questions generated from the weapon module content: the function of each part, naming a part from its photo, the direction a part moves during disassembly and which step follows another (plus the theory questions above). Wrong answers are taken from the other steps of the same weapon; parts that look alike in a photo (the pins) are never offered as each other's wrong answer. Steps whose movement is an action rather than a direction (unhook, unscrew) get no direction question.
